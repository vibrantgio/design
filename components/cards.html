Cards, groups & elevation

The two are one ruling with two answers, and choosing between them answers one question: am I dividing the page, or singling something out? A card singles something out — .card, a rounded surface raised on the content by its fill: the platform's grouped box, --platform-card-fill, a small step of fill away from the content and never a cast shadow, because a card cannot float or leave. A group divides the page — .group, a hairline around related components at the level of the surface the group is in, taking that surface's own fill. Both carry --radius-lg corners, a --space-4 inset and --space-3 gaps between the stacked slots. Neither styles its slot text: a card draws no text at all, and a group draws only its own label.

The card — .card

One rounded surface in --platform-card-fill, a small step of fill away from the content it stands on, and that fill is the whole of how it singles its content out: no line of its own, no shadow, never wearing a role. What you want to say about a card is a .badge in its header.

The group — .group

A hairline and nothing else: the group declares no background, so it takes the fill of the surface it is in and what it holds stands on that surface. The line is --platform-separator, the seam two regions sharing one fill are parted by — less pronounced than the 3:1 mark a graphic carrying meaning owes, and never transparent, because it is all that says where the group ends. The optional label is .group-label, top-leading inside the hairline. A group may hold a card; it never holds another group, and it wears no role.

The levels

A level is not a fill source: each level is filled with the platform's own name for what stands there. The backdrop is the window's plane, the content is the text background, the chrome is the sidebar material, a raised surface is the grouped box's fill, and a floating one stands on the window's plane again. A card on the content is raised; a group lives at whatever level it is already in and moves nothing.

The opt-in shadow

The dp shadows are --shadow-N for floating transients only — menus, dialogs, toasts, tooltips: a float adds box-shadow: var(--shadow-N) on top of the platform fill its level is given. Resting surfaces — every card among them — use the fill alone.

Dark mode

Nothing here is mode-specific: a class="dark" wrapper flips the platform variables, and every level is filled from them — the card's fill and the group's hairline alike.
