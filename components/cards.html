Cards, groups & elevation

The two are one ruling with two answers, and choosing between them answers one question: am I dividing the page, or singling something out? A card singles something out — .card, a rounded surface raised on the content by tonal elevation: a raised surface separates from the surface it stands on by colour, one neutral-ramp step per level, never by a cast shadow, because a card cannot float or leave. A group divides the page — .group, a hairline around related components at the level of the surface the group is in, taking that surface's own fill. Both carry --radius-lg corners, a --space-4 inset and --space-3 gaps between the stacked slots. Neither styles its slot text: a card draws no text at all, and a group draws only its own label.

The card — .card

One rounded surface at --elevation-1, one step above the app's --color-bg plane, and the raise is the whole of how it singles its content out: no line of its own, never outlined, never wearing a role. The only border it carries is the seam its raise owes — var(--ground-seam, var(--elevation-0-seam)), transparent in the scheme where the fill tells the raise on its own and a hairline where it does not. What you want to say about a card is a .badge in its header.

The group — .group

A hairline and nothing else: the group declares no background, so it takes the fill of the surface it is in and what it holds stands on that surface. The line is var(--ground-hairline, var(--elevation-0-hairline)), the seam two regions sharing one fill are parted by — quieter than the 3:1 mark a graphic carrying meaning owes, and never transparent, because it is all that says where the group ends. The optional label is .group-label, top-leading inside the hairline. A group may hold a card; it never holds another group, and it wears no role.

The elevation

Each step is a surface fill: --elevation-0 is the bg pin, levels 1–3 are neutral steps 200/300/400, resolved as var() references into the ramp so they flip with dark mode. It stops at 3 — desktop has no six-level stack. A card on the content lives at 1; a group lives at whatever level it is already in and moves nothing.

The opt-in shadow

The dp shadows survive as --shadow-N for floating transients only — menus, dialogs, toasts, tooltips: a float adds box-shadow: var(--shadow-N) on top of its tonal fill. Resting surfaces — every card among them — use the fill alone.

Dark mode

Nothing here is mode-specific: a class="dark" wrapper flips the colour variables, and because --elevation-N is a reference into the neutral ramp, every level flips with them — the card's raise and the group's hairline alike.
