Overlays

The transient surfaces: the scrimmed dialog (.scrim + .dialog), the anchored popover (.popover + .popover-tail), the inverse-video tooltip (.tooltip) and the floating toast (.toast, stacked in a .toast-stack). All four float, so all four stand on the window's own plane (--platform-window-background) inside a separator hairline, and the cast shadow is what says they float: the scrim isolates the dialog, the popover points at its anchor with a tail, the toast leaves by itself, and the tooltip is the smallest of them.

The decision dialog

A decision is a question you must answer. It mandates right-aligned footer actions in a .dialog-footer ending in the default that answers Return, binds Escape to Cancel, and shows no close X anywhere. Its scrim is inert: the backdrop absorbs every press and answers none of them, because dismissal is itself one of the answers and a stray click must not give it. When the primary is destructive — as here — the Return-bound default moves to Cancel: Return may never reach a destructive action.

The dismissable panel

A panel is a place you opened and can leave. It mandates a ghost close X top-right (.btn.ghost.icon in the .dialog-header), a scrim click that dismisses, and Escape likewise; it claims no keys of its own — Return belongs to whatever holds focus inside it. A panel has no footer: its changes apply live, which is what makes leaving free.

The focus-trapped state

While a dialog is open, focus is trapped: Tab and Shift+Tab cycle only the dialog's own focusables — the close X first, then the footer actions — and never reach the content under the scrim. Each focusable draws its own ring; the dialog wraps and decorates nothing. Shown with the forcing twin .is-focus, which shares the live :focus-visible declarations.

Popover

The unscrimmed anchored surface: content plus a --space-3 inset over the window's own plane with a 1 px --platform-separator stroke, radius --radius-md, and a .popover-tail — a 12×6 px triangle in the surface's own fill — bridging the --space-2 gap to the anchor. The tail modifier names the placement: a .top popover sits above its anchor, tail pointing down. No shadow: an unscrimmed transient separates by fill alone.

Tooltip

The inverse-video bubble: --platform-label as fill under a label in --platform-control-background, label-small metrics, radius --radius-sm, --space-2/--space-1 padding, one --space-1 gap from its trigger. It takes no fill from the elevation levels and no shadow — at tooltip size, inversion is the whole cue. It appears after a dwell and only one is visible per window.

Toast

One queued notification: 240 px wide with a 36 px legibility floor that deliberately does not follow density — a toast is a message, not a control — label-medium text in --platform-label over --platform-window-background, which is what every floating surface on this platform is filled with, floating on the measured --platform-floating-shadow. The status is carried on the leading edge alone, one --space-2 wide, in the platform's system colour for it: .success, .warning and .error swap that colour and the default is systemBlue. A toast says one thing about one event, and the colour of that edge is the whole of what it says; the message stays in the reading colour every other surface uses. Toasts stack in a corner-anchored .toast-stack, newest nearest the anchored edge.

Dark mode

Nothing here is mode-specific except the scrim, which deliberately never flips: --platform-scrim is black at 50% alpha in both modes, because a scrim dims by reducing luminance. Every fill, stroke and tint is a token reference, so a class="dark" wrapper flips the lot.
