Overlays

The transient surfaces: the scrimmed dialog (.scrim + .dialog), the anchored popover (.popover + .popover-tail), the inverse-video tooltip (.tooltip) and the floating toast (.toast, stacked in a .toast-stack). The elevation grammar separates them: a scrimmed dialog sits at --elevation-2 because the scrim, not the fill, isolates it; the unscrimmed, shadowless popover separates by fill alone and takes the deepest --elevation-3; the toast floats and can leave, so it alone keeps a cast shadow (--shadow-3) over its tinted level-2 base; the tooltip takes no storey at all — it inverts, Text ground under a Surface label, the stronger cue at that size.

The decision dialog

A decision is a question you must answer. It mandates right-aligned footer actions in a .dialog-footer ending in the default that answers Return, binds Escape to Cancel, and shows no close X anywhere. Its scrim is inert: the backdrop absorbs every press and answers none of them, because dismissal is itself one of the answers and a stray click must not give it. When the primary is destructive — as here — the Return-bound default moves to Cancel: Return may never reach a destructive action.

The dismissable panel

A panel is a place you opened and can leave. It mandates a ghost close X top-right (.btn.ghost.icon in the .dialog-header), a scrim click that dismisses, and Escape likewise; it claims no keys of its own — Return belongs to whatever holds focus inside it. A panel has no footer: its changes apply live, which is what makes leaving free.

The focus-trapped state

While a dialog is open, focus is trapped: Tab and Shift+Tab cycle only the dialog's own focusables — the close X first, then the footer actions — and never reach the content under the scrim. Each focusable draws its own ring; the dialog wraps and decorates nothing. Shown with the forcing twin .is-focus, which shares the live :focus-visible declarations.

Popover

The unscrimmed anchored surface: content plus a --space-3 inset over the deepest level-3 fill with the 1 px neutral-500 stroke, radius --radius-md, and a .popover-tail — a 12×6 px triangle in the surface's own fill — bridging the --space-2 gap to the anchor. The tail modifier names the placement: a .top popover sits above its anchor, tail pointing down. No shadow: an unscrimmed transient separates by fill alone.

Tooltip

The inverse-video bubble: --color-text as ground under a label in --color-surface, label-small metrics, radius --radius-sm, --space-2/--space-1 padding, one --space-1 gap from its trigger. It takes no elevation storey and no shadow — at tooltip size, inversion is the whole cue. It appears after a dwell and only one is visible per window.

Toast

One queued notification: 240 px wide with a 36 px legibility floor that deliberately does not follow density — a toast is a message, not a control — label-medium text over a level-2 base tinted 20% with the level accent, ringed by the accent outline, and floating on --shadow-3: the one overlay that keeps its cast shadow, because on dark grounds the shadow is what separates it. Level modifiers .success, .warning, .error swap the accent; the default is info on the accent pin. Toasts stack in a corner-anchored .toast-stack, newest nearest the anchored edge.

Dark mode

Nothing here is mode-specific except the scrim, which deliberately never flips: --color-scrim is black at 50% alpha in both modes, because a scrim dims by reducing luminance. Every fill, stroke and tint is a token reference, so a class="dark" wrapper flips the lot.
