Buttons & badges

The button is .btn — filled by default, with .tonal and .ghost selecting the less pronounced emphasis variants. Emphasis is a colour property and nothing else: every variant keeps the same size and the same focus halo. There is no size prop — heights come from --density-control-height, and density (.compact on any subtree) is the size axis. States shown statically wear the forcing twins (.is-hover, .is-active, .is-focus) that the token sheet groups into the live pseudo-class rules; disabled uses the native attribute.

Filled — .btn

The most pronounced variant: the default push button's own measured fill under the text that reads on it. One per surface — the action the screen is about. Under the pointer the platform's hover overlay composites over that fill and held its press overlay does, a press winning over a hover. Focus keeps the fill and lays the platform's keyboard focus indicator on the button's own outline at --focus-halo-width, half the band past the box and half over it. Disabled is the platform's fade: the push button's own fill at --disabled-coverage over the surface the button stands on, inside the separator at the same coverage, under the platform's disabled control text.

Ghost — .btn.ghost

The least pronounced variant: no fill at rest, the platform's control text, and the platform's own overlay composited straight onto whatever it stands on under the pointer — which is the only fill a ghost ever has. For affordances that must be present without being the subject — a dialog's close X, a toolbar of icons. A ghost is less pronounced, not smaller: full height, full focus halo.

Toolbar control — .btn.chrome

The platform's bordered toolbar control: a capsule at --density-toolbar-control-height — the band's own measured 36 px against a dialog control's 24 — cornered at half of it, filled with --platform-toolbar-control-fill inside --platform-toolbar-control-rim, casting the measured drop shadow that tells a light control from a light band. It is the one control on this platform that tints under the pointer. A chrome control belongs in a chrome region: one standing on the content plane is a defect in the page that put it there, so the specimen below stands on a band.

Compact density

The same markup inside a class="compact" wrapper: --density-control-height drops from 24 to 19 px and the padding follows. Density is the size axis — there is no small or large button. A control's pointer target is the control: what it draws is what a pointer has to land on, so compact shrinks the target with the pixels.

Badges — .badge

The system's own word about a thing, set inline at label-medium metrics over a field of the platform's own system colour, with the text that reads on it — the platform paints a status disc in the system colour itself and never a tint of it. No boundary and no vertical padding, so the height is the role's line box; the side padding is --space-2 and the corner is --radius-base, deliberately not the pill a chip wears. A badge beside a button is a fraction of that button's height and is meant to be. The default is the plain category label. A badge speaks as a word or as a count; the close mark a dismissible one carries is a Gio interaction and has no class in this sheet.

Status badges — .badge.success / .badge.warning / .badge.error / .badge.info

The four statuses, differing from the default and from each other in hue alone: there is no emphasis axis, because emphasis belongs where interaction does. Each is the platform's own system colour — green, orange, red, blue — under the text that reads on an accent fill. Nothing is derived: the pair is the platform's. Status is vocabulary: compose these; never inline-style a status colour.
